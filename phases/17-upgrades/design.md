# 17 · Design

## Battery power (M49)

Stage 01 stays as built: the ALITOVE 24 V adapter, e-stop and lever connectors. This upgrade adds a battery box that
plugs into the **same** screw-terminal jack in place of the adapter, so only one source is ever connected.

### Three ways to power the machine

| Source | Bus | Runs for | Parts |
|---|---|---|---|
| Wall adapter (stage 01) | 24.0 V | unlimited | PWR-30 (have) |
| USB-C power bank straight in (Level 1) | 20.0 V | bank Wh × 0.85 ÷ 12.9 W typical [TBD: measure, T-B4] | BAT-02 PD trigger, BAT-06 plug lead, any bank with a 20 V profile, ≥ 45 W |
| 24 V lithium-ion (Li-ion) pack (Level 2) | 24.0 → 20.4 V | ≈ 6 h typical, ≈ 2.5 h at peak (estimate: 108 Wh × 0.8 × 0.9 ÷ 12.9 W / 31 W) [TBD: measure, T-B4] | BAT-01 to BAT-06 |

Level 2 charges from **any** USB-C Power Delivery (USB-PD) source with a 20 V profile: the laptop charger, a wall
charger or the power bank. It keeps running while it charges: the charging module feeds the machine and the pack together.

Everything on the bus accepts 20–24 V: SKR Pico 12–24 V, NLDD-350H 9–56 V, PC817 module 18–24 V, STA-03 buck 18–24 V.
A full 6-cell Li-ion pack (6S) reads 25.2 V, above the motor board's 24 V rating, so the pack is only ever charged to
24.0 V (4.0 V a cell: about 80 % of capacity, gentler on the cells). Its own 25.2 V wall charger is never used.

### Power path (Level 2)

```
USB-C charger / bank ─► BAT-02 PD trigger (20 V) ─► BAT-03 charging module, constant voltage (CV) 24.00 V, constant current (CC) 1.000 A ─┐
                                                                                                                          ├─► node ─► BAT-06 plug ─► stage 01 jack ─► BAT-05 INA219 ─► e-stop ─► limit switch ─► SKR Pico
BAT-01 pack (its own Battery Management System, BMS) ─► BAT-04 fuse 3.15 A slow-blow ─────────────────────────────────────┘
```

- One plug, one source: the battery box's plug lead (BAT-06) goes where the adapter's plug went. The adapter and the pack are never connected together.
- Plug in with the e-stop **pressed**, then release it: the e-stop contacts take the surge into the motor board's capacitors, not the jack.
- The power monitor (BAT-05) sits right after the jack, so it reads every source, the wall adapter included: bus volts, load current and energy used, reported by the hub.
- Off switch: the e-stop (motors and lamp) or unplugging the lead (everything). Charging carries on with either.

Level 1 needs only BAT-02 and BAT-06, plus BAT-05 and BAT-05b for the monitor. The INA219 chains on the hub's first
I²C pair: hub → AS7341 (SPC-02 cable) → INA219 (BAT-05b) → OLED. Its address (0x40) is clear of the AS7341 (0x39) and
the OLED (0x3C). Without the AS7341, plug it in with an SPC-02 cable (Adafruit 4397, out of stock on 2026-11-04).

### Hub firmware

`firmware/hub/main.py` reads the INA219 once a second and keeps the source (WALL, PACK or USBC) in `source.txt`.

| Line | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `SOURCE PACK` / `SOURCE USBC 74` / `SOURCE WALL` | send after a full charge: sets the source (and the bank's rated Wh), resets energy used |
| `BATT?` → `BATT PACK 23.10 0.412 9.5 1.20 OK` | source, volts, amps, watts, Wh used, state |
| `BATT … LOW` / `BATT … CRIT` (sent on change) | CRIT also sends `BTN PAUSE`: the run stops after the pose in progress |

| Source | LOW | CRIT |
|---|---|---|
| PACK | < 21.6 V (3.6 V a cell) | < 20.4 V (3.4 V a cell, above the BMS cut-off) |
| USBC | < 15 % of the rated Wh left | < 5 % left, or the bank stops holding 20 V (< 18.5 V) |
| WALL | < 22.0 V (adapter sagging) | never |

The thresholds live in `battery_state()` and are tested in `software/tests/test_hub_battery.py`.
